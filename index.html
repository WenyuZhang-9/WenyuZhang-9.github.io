About

Wenyu Zhang is a PhD student in Urban and Regional Science at Texas A&M University (GPA 4.0/4.0), advised by Dr. Siyu Yu and Dr. Xinyue Ye. Her research bridges GeoAI, remote sensing, and urban planning to understand how cities transform, how digital systems can support spatial reasoning, and how people adapt to environmental and hazard-related pressures.

Her recent work spans urban village transformation in China, knowledge-aware digital twins, geospatial annotation, campus heat stress forecasting, and GeoAI-based assessment of heat adaptation behavior. She is interested in building spatially grounded and socially meaningful analytical frameworks for resilient urban futures.

Projects

01

GeoAI for Urban Transformation

Measuring the disappearance, redevelopment, and functional transformation of urban spaces — with a focus on urban villages in China — through high-resolution remote sensing, image segmentation, and spatial data integration.

Related: [4] [6] · Under Review: Geospatial data for urban villages

02

Digital Twins and Spatial Reasoning

Developing knowledge-aware and graph-based retrieval systems for urban digital twins, enabling constraint-aware spatial reasoning, interpretable decision support, and natural-language querying of geographic entities.

Related: [5] [10]

03

Climate Adaptation and Mobility Behavior

Understanding how heat exposure and hazard contexts reshape urban behavior and mobility patterns, using GeoAI to assess where and how adaptation emerges spatially across different urban environments.

Related: Working paper (Shops, Shelters, and Survival)

04

Geospatial Annotation and Marking

Advancing methods for annotating and interpreting complex urban targets in remote sensing imagery, supporting scalable geospatial intelligence pipelines for urban governance and planning applications.

Related: [7]
