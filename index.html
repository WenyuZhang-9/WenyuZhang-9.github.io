About

Wenyu Zhang is a doctoral researcher in Urban and Regional Science at Texas A&M University. Her work connects urban planning, GIScience, remote sensing, and artificial intelligence to study how cities transform, how digital systems can support spatial reasoning, and how people adapt to environmental and hazard-related pressures.

Her recent scholarship spans urban village transformation, knowledge-aware digital twins, geospatial annotation, campus heat stress forecasting, and GeoAI-based assessment of heat adaptation behavior. Across these topics, she is interested in building spatially grounded and socially meaningful analytical frameworks for resilient urban futures.

Research Themes

Three connected directions define my current work.

GeoAI for Urban Transformation

Measuring the disappearance, redevelopment, and functional transformation of urban spaces through high-resolution imagery, spatial data, and intelligent interpretation.

Digital Twins and Spatial Reasoning

Developing knowledge-aware and graph-based retrieval systems that make urban digital twins more transparent, contextual, and useful for spatial decision-making.

Climate Adaptation and Mobility

Understanding how heat exposure and hazard contexts reshape urban behavior, mobility adaptation, and the predictability of human movement.
