Research Agenda

Spatial intelligence for changing, adaptive, and resilient cities.

My research connects GeoAI, digital twins, and GIScience with substantive urban questions about transformation, adaptation, and the unequal spatial consequences of environmental stress.

Related PublicationsAcademic CV

Core Research Directions

Three interconnected domains organize my current work.

Direction 01

GeoAI for urban transformation and spatial change

I investigate how urban spaces disappear, persist, and transform over time, with a particular focus on informal settlements, redevelopment trajectories, and the measurement of physical and functional urban change.

This line of work combines high-resolution remote sensing, image segmentation, multi-source urban data, and spatial interpretation to move beyond static mapping toward dynamic understanding of urban transformation.

  • Urban village identification, demolition, and land-use transformation.
  • Geospatial annotation and visual interpretation for complex urban targets.
  • Remote-sensing-based measurement of spatial change in support of planning and urban governance.
Direction 02

Digital twins, graph retrieval, and spatial reasoning

I develop knowledge-aware and retrieval-augmented systems for digital twins, especially where spatial reasoning requires constraints, context, and explainable links between textual questions and geographic entities.

Rather than treating a digital twin as a static visualization layer, I approach it as an interactive knowledge environment that can support interpretable, constraint-aware decision assistance.

  • Graph-based retrieval for campus digital twins.
  • Constraint-aware reasoning across spatial, temporal, and accessibility contexts.
  • Human-centered interfaces for querying and exploring urban knowledge systems.
Direction 03

Climate adaptation, heat exposure, and mobility behavior

I study how people adapt their movement and activity patterns under environmental stress, with recent work examining heat adaptation behavior in San Antonio and broader interests in mobility predictability under hazard conditions.

This direction integrates mobility data, urban context, and GeoAI to ask how adaptation emerges spatially and how resilience may be unevenly distributed across people and places.

  • GeoAI-based assessment of heat adaptation behavior.
  • Mobility predictability and behavioral stability under hazard contexts.
  • Connections between environmental exposure, urban form, and adaptive opportunity.

Methods & Analytical Toolkit

My work is methodologically plural, but consistently grounded in spatial reasoning and urban questions.

GeoAI & Remote Sensing

Semantic segmentation, multi-temporal image analysis, spatial visual interpretation, and large-scale urban scene understanding.

Spatial Data Science

GIS, network analysis, geospatial databases, urban indicators, and multimodal integration of remote-sensing and social sensing data.

Knowledge Systems

Graph retrieval, digital twins, question answering, LLM-based interfaces, and interpretable spatial decision support.
